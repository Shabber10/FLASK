## 1. What is Database Querying & Filtering in Plain English?

Imagine you have a database containing 50,000 products. 

A user doesn't want to look through all 50,000 items at once. They want to ask specific questions:
*   *"Show me only products in the 'Electronics' category."*
*   *"Show me products priced between $50 and $200."*
*   *"Show me products rated 4 stars or higher."*

**Querying** is the process of asking questions to the database. **Filtering** is adding rules (`WHERE` clauses) to narrow down the results to match the user's request.

---

## 2. Pattern Matching: Searching for Partial Text (`LIKE` vs `ILIKE`)

When a user types `"phone"` into a search bar, they expect to find:
*   `"Smart Phone"`
*   `"Headphones"`
*   `"PHONE ACCESSORIES"`

In SQL, searching for partial text inside strings is called **Pattern Matching**:
*   **`LIKE`**: Case-sensitive search. Searching for `'phone'` will **NOT** match `'PHONE'`.
*   **`ILIKE`**: **Case-insensitive search** (the "i" stands for insensitive). Searching for `'phone'` matches `'Phone'`, `'PHONE'`, and `'pHoNe'`!

> **Jinja / SQLAlchemy Syntax**: `Product.name.ilike('%phone%')` (The `%` symbol acts as a wildcard meaning "match any characters before or after").

## 3. What is Pagination and Why is it Crucial?

Imagine what would happen if Google returned 5,000,000 search results on a single web page:
1.  Your browser would freeze or crash trying to render millions of HTML elements.
2.  The web server would run out of memory trying to fetch millions of database rows at once.

**Pagination** solves this problem by breaking large result sets into small, manageable pages (e.g., 10 items per page):

```
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
| Page 1 of 500  (Items 1 to 10)                              |
| +---------------------------------------------------------+ |
| | Product 1 ... Product 10                                | |
| +---------------------------------------------------------+ |
| [Prev]  [1]  2  3  4  5 ... 500  [Next]                     |
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
```

Flask-SQLAlchemy provides a built-in helper called **`db.paginate()`** that automatically handles page numbers, total pages, and Next/Prev buttons!

## 4. What are Aggregations & Grouping?

Sometimes, instead of fetching individual rows, you want to calculate summary statistics across data:
*   **Count**: *"How many total products do we sell?"* (`func.count()`)
*   **Average**: *"What is the average product price in Electronics?"* (`func.avg()`)
*   **Max / Min**: *"What is the highest and lowest price?"* (`func.max()`, `func.min()`)

Combining aggregations with **`GROUP BY`** lets you calculate stats per category (e.g., Average price for Books vs Average price for Apparel).

---

## 5. What is a Savepoint? (Nested Transactions)

Imagine placing an online order involving 3 steps:
1.  Deduct $100 from your bank account.
2.  Reserve item stock in warehouse.
3.  Generate shipping label.

What happens if Step 3 fails? You want to undo Step 3 without cancelling the whole order! 

A **Savepoint** (`db.session.begin_nested()`) creates a temporary checkpoint inside a database transaction. If an error occurs, you can rollback to that checkpoint without losing earlier work!
